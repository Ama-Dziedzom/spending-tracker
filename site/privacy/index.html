What LogIt collects

Your account

  • Name you enter at sign-up. LogIt also uses it to spot money you move between your own accounts (for example, a transfer “from Ama Barnor” to your MoMo).
  • Phone number, which you sign in with. We confirm it by texting you a one-time code.
  • Email address, only if you have an older email login or add one for account recovery. Your password is handled by our login provider and is not stored by LogIt in readable form.
  • Your settings: avatar choice (an illustration or your initials; we never ask for your gender), notification choices, whether widgets hide amounts, whether “Help LogIt read more banks” is on, and whether Ask LogIt chat answers are on.

Your money alerts and transactions

  • The text of each alert LogIt logs, kept with the transaction it created, so you can see exactly what LogIt read and the same message is never counted twice. When a provider sends two messages for one payment (a notice and a confirmation), both are kept with that payment.
  • What LogIt reads from each alert: amount, whether money came in or went out, the shop or person, the category, any fee or tax, the balance after, the provider’s transaction ID, and the date and time.
  • Alerts LogIt couldn’t read. These wait in Check with their full text so you can add them yourself in a few taps.
  • Senders LogIt learned. When you confirm an alert from a bank LogIt didn’t know, it remembers that message’s wording, with amounts, names, numbers and dates replaced by placeholders, plus the wallet you chose. The next alert like it is then logged for you.
  • Your wallets (for example MTN MoMo or a bank account), their names and balances.
  • What you add or change yourself: cash you log, transactions you add by hand, category changes (so LogIt gets that shop right next time), weekly spending limits, and income you tell it to expect.
  • Your recent Ask LogIt questions: the words of your last 20 questions, so you can ask them again. The answers are not stored.

Technical information

  • Server records. Like any online service, LogIt’s server keeps a short technical log of requests: the time, your account ID, your IP address and whether the request worked. For messages LogIt skipped or couldn’t read, the log also holds a short preview (up to 80 characters) with amounts, names, phone numbers, dates and references replaced by placeholders. Logs can include the amount of a logged payment. We use these logs only to keep LogIt working and fix problems.
  • App updates. When LogIt opens, it checks for updates. That check sends the app version, your phone’s platform, a random installation ID and your IP address to our update provider (Expo). It does not include your account or money data.

Kept only on your phone

  • Your login session, so you stay signed in.
  • A small summary for your widgets (today’s and this week’s totals, your latest few entries, wallet balances, and how many messages need checking). It is removed when you log out. Lock-screen widgets hide amounts by default.
  • Alerts that arrived while you were offline (up to 100), until LogIt can send them. These are cleared when you log out.
  • If you turn on Face ID or fingerprint unlock with an email login, your email and password are saved in your phone’s secure storage (the iPhone Keychain or Android Keystore). Your face and fingerprint never leave your phone’s own security system; LogIt never sees them.

How alerts reach LogIt

On Android, if you allow it, LogIt receives new text messages as they arrive. A check runs on your phone first, and only messages that look like money alerts are sent to LogIt: a message must mention an amount in cedis and come from a known MoMo or bank sender, or read like a transaction alert. Messages with one-time codes are ignored. Everything else never leaves your phone. LogIt does not read messages already in your inbox. You can turn this off in LogIt’s settings at any time.

On iPhone, Apple doesn’t let apps read your messages. You choose to send them to LogIt yourself, in one of three ways:

  • Autopilot: a Shortcuts automation you set up, which sends messages containing “GHS” to LogIt. Because iPhone can only filter by those words, a chat that mentions GHS can reach LogIt too. LogIt’s server checks each message and discards anything that isn’t a provider alert without saving it (only the short, masked log preview described above is kept).
  • Share sheet: you share a message to LogIt.
  • Paste: you paste a message into the app.

Whichever way it arrives, the message is sent securely (encrypted in transit) to LogIt’s server, which reads it and saves the result to your account.

Voice questions. If you use the mic in Ask LogIt, your phone’s own speech service (Apple’s on iPhone, Google’s on Android) turns your words into text. Depending on your phone, that may happen on Apple’s or Google’s servers under their privacy policies. LogIt only receives the text and never records or keeps audio.

How LogIt uses your information

Only to run LogIt for you:

  • turning alerts into transactions and keeping wallet balances up to date
  • showing your spending, insights, limits and answers to your questions
  • spotting duplicates and transfers between your own accounts
  • sending you LogIt’s own notifications, for example “Logged · GHS 85 on Food”, or a heads-up when you’re near a weekly limit
  • signing you in, keeping your account secure, and fixing problems

Under Act 843, we process your data because you asked us to provide this service and, for message reading and Ask LogIt chat answers, because you gave your consent. You can withdraw that consent at any time (see Your choices).

Helping LogIt read more banks (off unless you turn it on)

If you turn on Help LogIt read more banks, then when LogIt can’t read an alert it also keeps a masked template of the message: the provider’s wording with every amount, name, phone number, account number, date, time and reference replaced by placeholders such as {amt} and {name}. I review these templates to teach LogIt new message formats for everyone. They are deleted with your account, and you can turn this off at any time in Settings.

Dates of MTN MoMo messages

Some MTN MoMo messages don’t include a date. Because MTN numbers its transactions in order, LogIt keeps a list of MTN transaction IDs and the times they happened, and uses it to estimate when undated messages happened. This list holds only the transaction ID and the time: no account, name, phone number or amount, so it can’t be linked back to you. It is shared across all users and is kept when you delete your account.

Ask LogIt

Ask LogIt answers questions like “How much did I spend on food this week?” It works in one of two ways, and you choose. LogIt asks you before the first chat answer, and you can change your choice later in Settings.

On your phone only. LogIt works out the answer from your data on your phone. Nothing about your money is sent anywhere to answer the question.

Chat answers (if you turn them on). Your question, the earlier questions in that chat, and a summary of your money built on your phone are sent through LogIt’s server to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI model, which writes the answer.

The summary includes your totals by month and category, your wallet names and balances, and recent entries with the shop or person’s name, amount, category and date. It does not include the text of your messages, phone numbers, account numbers or references.

Anthropic handles this data under its commercial terms as our service provider. It does not use it to train its AI models, and it deletes it within 30 days (longer only if needed to enforce its usage policy or to comply with the law). LogIt does not store the answers.

Where your data is stored

Some of these providers store data outside Ghana, including in the United States. Where that happens, we choose providers that encrypt data in transit and at rest and only process it on our instructions, so your data stays protected to the standard Act 843 expects.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and money data: until you delete it or delete your account. We don’t delete inactive accounts automatically.
  • Alerts in Check (including ones you dismiss) and senders LogIt learned: until you delete your account.
  • Server logs: kept by Railway for up to 90 days, then deleted automatically. They can’t be deleted earlier.
  • Ask LogIt chat answers: not stored by LogIt. Anthropic deletes what it received within 30 days.
  • Database backups: [kept for up to 7 days / not kept on our current plan], then overwritten.
  • Masked templates (if you turned that setting on): until you delete your account.
  • MTN transaction ID and time list: kept, as it isn’t linked to anyone.
  • On your phone: until you log out or uninstall LogIt.

Your choices and rights

Under Act 843 you can ask to see the personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, and object to how we use it. You can do most of this yourself in the app:

  • See and correct: every transaction shows the original alert text. You can edit or delete any transaction, and change your name or phone number in Settings › Profile.
  • Export: Settings › Help & privacy › Export my transactions gives you a CSV file.
  • Stop message reading on Android: turn off “Read SMS alerts” in LogIt’s settings, or turn off SMS for LogIt in your phone’s Settings › Apps › LogIt › Permissions.
  • Stop autopilot on iPhone: delete or turn off the LogIt automation in the Shortcuts app.
  • Turn off Ask LogIt chat answers: Settings. Answers are then worked out on your phone only.
  • Turn off Help LogIt read more banks: Settings.
  • Delete everything: see below.

In the app: Settings › Profile › Delete account, then type DELETE to confirm. This permanently removes your account and everything in it. Full details and how to delete without the app.

Keeping your data safe

  • All data moves between the app and our servers over encrypted connections (HTTPS).
  • Each person’s data is locked to their own account in the database, so one user can never read another’s.
  • You can lock LogIt with Face ID or your fingerprint.
  • Sign-in uses one-time codes, not reusable passwords.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach ever affects your data, I’ll tell you and the Data Protection Commission as soon as I can.

Children

LogIt is for people aged 18 and over. I don’t knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you think a child has created an account, contact me and I’ll delete it.
